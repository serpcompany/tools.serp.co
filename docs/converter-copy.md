# Converter copy

How the copy on converter and compressor pages is written, checked and
applied (#356, parent #355): the page title, H1 and meta description, the
subtitle, the FAQs, the format cards and the pair notes. It covers every live
`convert` and `compress` Tool, about 2,200 pages. Downloader Landers and other
operations have their own copy and are out of scope, and so are the 8
transcription Tools (audio or video to `txt`, `isTranscriptionTool` in
`apps/tools/lib/metadata.ts`), which the owner names separately.

## Where copy lives

Copy stays in `apps/tools/lib/catalog/tools.json`, under each Tool's `content`
(owner decision, 2026-10-21). Writers never edit that file. They write a
batch file, `apps/tools/content/batches/<batch>.json`, and
`pnpm content:apply <batch>` writes it into the registry:

```json
{
  "webp-to-png": {
    "seo": { "title": "…", "h1": "…", "description": "…", "howToHeading": "…" },
    "tool": { "subtitle": "…" },
    "faqs": [{ "question": "…", "answer": "…" }],
    "aboutSection": { "title": "…", "fromFormat": {}, "toFormat": {} },
    "pairNotes": { "title": "…", "points": ["…"] }
  }
}
```

Each field a batch gives replaces that field of the Tool's content; `tool` is
merged, and a batch sets only its `subtitle` (no page shows `tool.title`). Content a Tool
already has and the batch doesn't name (its video, related Tools) is kept. A
Tool without content gets the page it showed before: its name as fallback
title, its file filter (`converterAccept`). `content:apply` refuses unknown,
retired or non-converter ids, and writes nothing unless the whole catalog then
passes `pnpm content:check`. It rewrites only the named Tools' entries, in the
file's own layout (2-space JSON, ASCII with `\u` escapes), so a re-run
changes nothing.

## Titles, H1s and descriptions

A title follows how people search the Tool, not one pattern for every page
(owner decisions, 2026-10-22). Ahrefs shows each operation searched three
ways: "png to pdf", "convert png to pdf", "png to pdf converter" (and "how to
convert png to pdf"); "compress png", "png compressor", "how to compress a
png". So each page uses all three shapes:

- **Title**: the verb phrase, built on the biggest variant among the keyword
  and the alias keywords in the brief.
- **H1**: the noun phrase. Take the noun from the keyword or an alias
  (converter, compressor, optimizer, generator); keep the keyword in it.
- **How-to heading**: the top "how to" question. It is generated, as below,
  unless the batch sets `seo.howToHeading` (optional): use the top question
  from the brief's questions when there is one.

`pnpm content:brief` lists each Tool's top 8 matching terms and top 5
questions, with volumes, from `apps/tools/benchmarks/keyword-ideas.json`
(Ahrefs; the brief names its source). The title keeps the head keyword and
works in the biggest term where it fits; the How-to heading uses the top "how
to" question; the FAQs answer the listed questions, not invented ones. When
the status CSV has no keyword (the compressors), the brief uses the file's
head keyword and volume. A Tool the file lacks shows "No variant data": use
the rules above without it.

With no `content.seo`, `toolSeo` in `apps/tools/lib/metadata.ts` generates the
default from the Tool's name, and the layout adds " | SERP Tools":

| Page | Title | H1 | How-to heading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Converter | `Convert {A} to {B} Online (Free)` | `{A} to {B} Converter` | `How to convert {A} to {B}` |
| Compressor | `Compress {F} Files Online (Free)` | `{F} Compressor` | `How to compress a {F} file` |

The heading says "an" before a format read letter by letter from a vowel
sound ("an MP4", "an M4A", "an SVG") and "a" otherwise ("a PNG", "a MOV", "a
FLAC"). A title that would pass 60 characters with the layout's " | SERP
Tools" drops " Online (Free)": "Convert Markdown to Pandas DataFrame". A
converter whose name isn't a pair ("Character Counter") keeps its name as its
title. A compressor names its format as the format reference does ("WebP
Compressor"), whatever the catalog name's casing. Write your own title
in the same shape when the keyword has a noun the default lacks ("PNG
Optimizer", "to X online"); otherwise keep the default. A writer's
`content.seo` always wins: it replaces the generated title, H1 and
description, and the page's H1, its title and the `WebApplication` name all
follow it. The description is generated by `toolDescription` until a batch
writes one.

## Voice

- Write to the visitor, in plain US English: "your file", "you get".
- Say what happens, not how good it is. No "best", "fastest", "perfect",
  "high-quality", "seamless" or exclamation marks.
- Short sentences. One fact each.
- Name formats as people search them (WebP, PNG, MP4), and use the Tool's
  keyword once in the title, the H1 and the description.

## Lengths

- Title: at most 60 characters with the " | SERP Tools" the layout adds, so
  47 of your own. Never name the site.
- H1: at most 60 characters, and contains the keyword, worded as above.
- Description: 110 to 160 characters. Say what the page does, one fact that
  matters for the pair, and "free, with no sign-up" if room is left.
- How-to heading (optional): at most 70 characters, with each word of the
  keyword, in any order ("How do I convert a WebP to a PNG?").
- Subtitle: one sentence under the H1, about 80 characters.

## What each field is for

- **FAQs**: at least 3. Each answers a question a visitor of this pair asks
  before or after converting: what happens to transparency, animation, pages,
  sound, metadata or quality; why a result is bigger; what fails and why.
  Don't ask questions every page could answer ("Is it free?", "Is it safe?");
  the page and template already say so.
- **Pair notes**: a title and at least 2 points, shown in place of the About
  article: what to know before converting this pair, one fact per point.
- **Format cards** (`aboutSection`): a card for each format, required on a
  converter. Take names, full names and facts from the format reference
  (`apps/tools/content/formats.json`, #357) once it exists.
- **How-to** (`howTo`, optional): a title and at least 3 steps, in place of
  the generated How-to. The title follows the How-to heading's rules, and
  `seo.howToHeading` is then left out. A page written in depth doesn't
  write one (owner decision, 2026-10-22): the generated How-to stays.
- **Guide** (`guide`, optional): a Markdown article after the pair notes,
  with `###` subheadings, paragraphs, bulleted lists and bold labels, and no
  tables. What it holds is under "Writing in depth".

### Writing in depth

A Tool in `apps/tools/benchmarks/content-topics.json` gets a longer page,
modeled on a file-extension reference page such as filext.com's (owner
decision, 2026-10-22). That file holds the Ahrefs topics and lengths of the
pages ranking for its keyword: a check on what visitors ask, not an outline
to copy. The page has at least 8 FAQs and a `guide` with these `###`
sections, in this order, written for the pair:

1. **What is a {FROM} file**: what it is, who made it, its history, and
   where people come across one.
2. **Programs that open {FROM} files**: grouped under Windows, Mac, Linux,
   web and mobile, one line on each program.
3. **{FROM} and {TO} compared**: extensions, media types, variants,
   compression, transparency, color depth and metadata, and how the two
   differ for this conversion.
4. **Other ways to convert {FROM} to {TO}**: the methods in
   `apps/tools/content/format-guides.json`, with their menu paths and limits.
5. **Problems with {FROM} files**: what goes wrong (won't open, wrong
   extension, too big, lost transparency) and the fix for each.

The FAQs follow the guide on the page. Aim the guide, FAQs, format cards and
pair notes together at 1,200 to 1,500 words. A page that can't reach that
with true, specific sentences stops short; say so instead of padding. Write
every page on its own: a format that appears on several pages (JPG, PDF)
gets a new angle for each pair, never a paragraph copied from another page.

## What may be claimed

Nothing on a page is false, and what the site does is always checkable:

- What the page does: the engine facts in the brief, read from the code (see
  "Briefs" below). If the brief says the code doesn't settle something, don't
  claim it either way.
- What a format is: the format reference.
- In a guide, a format's background, the programs that open it and other
  ways to convert: `apps/tools/content/format-guides.json` (each fact with
  its primary source), and the owner's wiki, `wiki.serp.co/file-types/<ext>/`,
  for facts and topics. Much of the wiki's text is copied word for word from
  FileInfo.com, so take its facts, never its wording: write every sentence
  fresh, in this page's structure and voice, with no sentence-by-sentence
  paraphrase and no list in the wiki's order with its notes. Leave out a wiki
  claim you know or find to be wrong, and a date, version or app-support
  claim you can't confirm.
- Outside a guide, no dates, release notes, version numbers, statistics,
  percentages, speeds, user counts or reviews. Converter pages have no
  changelog and no blog posts; `content:check` fails a Tool that has either.
- Never promise a result the engine can't give: "without losing quality" on a
  lossy compressor, "all pages" where only the first is converted, "stays on
  your device" where the file is uploaded (see `docs/execution-paths.md`).

## Links

Copy links only to live pages on this site, as a relative path
(`/png-to-jpg/`): an active Tool's page, a category page, `/categories/`,
`/brands/` or `/`. No `#` placeholders and no links off the site.

## The process

1. `pnpm content:brief <tool-id>… --name <batch>` writes
   `tmp/content-briefs/<batch>.md` (git-ignored), one self-contained brief for
   the writer. For each Tool: its keyword and alias keywords with volumes, its
   engine, where it runs and its last sweep, what the engine really does (with
   the FFmpeg command for media Tools), what the code doesn't settle, its
   format reference entries, its current copy and an entry to fill in. Once:
   this guide and a finished example.
2. The writer saves the batch as `apps/tools/content/batches/<batch>.json` and
   runs `pnpm content:check --batch <batch>`, which applies it in memory and
   writes nothing.
3. `pnpm content:apply <batch>` writes it. Commit the batch file with the
   tools.json change, so each Tool's copy has a reviewable source. A batch
   given as a path (`content:apply <file>.json`) resolves against
   `apps/tools`, where `pnpm -C apps/tools` runs the script.

The engine facts are in `apps/tools/scripts/lib/engine-facts.mjs`, each with
the file it was read from. When an engine changes, update its entry in the
same PR. A Tool whose sweep doesn't pass is flagged in its brief: write
nothing that promises it works.

## The checks

`pnpm content:check` runs inside `pnpm check` over every live converter and
compressor. It fails when:

- the title or H1 lacks the Tool's keyword (`keyword` in
  `apps/tools/benchmarks/tool-status.csv`; letters and digits compared, case
  and spaces ignored);
- the title is over 60 characters with " | SERP Tools", the H1 over 60, or
  the title names the site;
- a written How-to heading is over 70 characters, lacks a word of the keyword
  (of the Tool's formats when it has none), or isn't shown: the Tool isn't on
  the converter page, or its `content.howTo` replaces the section;
- the description is outside 110–160 characters (Ahrefs reports one under
  110 as too short);
- two Tools share a title, an H1 or a description;
- a link is `#`, empty, off the site, or to a path that isn't a live page; or a
  related Tool isn't live;
- a converter or compressor has `changelog` or `blogPosts`;
- for a Tool with `content.seo` (written to this guide): a FAQ answer is a
  near-duplicate of another Tool's, or of the template FAQs (one template
  Tool for each operation and each place the file is converted: in the
  browser or server first); or a required
  field is missing (subtitle, 3 FAQs, pair notes with 2 points, and format
  cards on a converter);
- for a Tool with a `guide` (written in depth): it has fewer than 8 FAQs; the
  guide's `###` headings aren't the five sections of "Writing in depth", in
  order (known by how they start or end: "What is", "Programs that",
  "compared", "Other ways to", "Problems with"); the guide has a table; or
  any field of its copy uses a common British spelling (colour, grey,
  licence, modelled, centre, behaviour, -ise and -isation, and others in
  `apps/tools/scripts/lib/content.mjs`).

Near-duplicate means a Jaccard similarity of 0.6 or more between the two
answers' three-word phrases, after lower-casing and replacing each Tool's own
format names with a placeholder. So "Your browser turns the WebP into a PNG"
and "Your browser turns the AVIF into a JPG" are one answer. Of the 551
curated answers in October 2026, 200 were word-for-word copies of another
Tool's, 291 scored under 0.3 and 60 fell between; the examples' answers score
0.04 at most. The rule covers only Tools with `content.seo`, since older
curated copy repeats across Tools and is replaced batch by batch.

## Examples

`apps/tools/content/batches/examples.json` holds two finished Tools, applied
with `content:apply`: `webp-to-png` (browser image engine: transparency kept,
first frame of an animation, metadata dropped) and `mp4-to-mp3` (FFmpeg in the
browser at 192 kbps, fails on a video without sound). Briefs include one of
them; copy their shape, not their sentences.
